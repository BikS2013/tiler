01 · What it is
A tiling window manager that lives in your menu bar.

Tiler arranges the windows of a desktop so they share the screen with no overlap and no wasted space. Each new window splits the one you were working in, along its longer side (a dwindle layout, as in Hyprland).

It works on one desktop of one display at a time, and only where you switch it on. Your other Spaces, your other screens and full-screen apps are never touched.

You keep using native macOS Spaces and Mission Control. Tiler just keeps the desktop you chose in order.

02 · Who it's for
For Mac users who want order on some desktops, not all of them.
01

Keyboard-first people

Developers, writers, analysts who live in a few windows side by side and would rather press a key than drag a corner.

Focus, swap and resize without the mouse.

02

A tiled workspace, a free one

Tile your coding or research desktop; keep a loose desktop for mail, chat and the odd floating window.

Each desktop is a separate choice.

03

Several displays

Tile the laptop screen while the external monitor stays freeform, or the other way round.

Tiler never moves a window on a screen you didn't turn on.

Not for you if you want every window on every desktop tiled automatically, or virtual workspaces that replace macOS Spaces.

03 · Why it feels light
Simple on purpose: fewer moving parts, fewer surprises.
0%
CPU while you work. Tiler only wakes up when a window opens, closes or moves, and goes fully idle on desktops that aren't tiled.
Pollingnone: it reacts to window events
Keyboard interceptionnone: system hotkeys only
Virtual workspacesnone: native Spaces
PermissionsAccessibility only
Cost of one update≈ 1 ms
Size of the code~2,900 lines
Measured on macOS 27 with two tiled desktops. Inspired by HyprMac (~35,000 lines), minus its workspaces, keyboard tap and polling.

05 · Turn it on, turn it off
Tiling is a switch per desktop, per display.
01
Menu bar: click the Tiler icon on the screen you want and choose Tile This Desktop. It acts on the display whose menu bar you clicked.
02
Keyboard: press ⌃⌘P on the display you're working on (the one with the focused window).
03
Off: the same item or key again. Windows simply stay where they are; Tiler stops all work there.
  • Remembered across restarts and logins.
  • The icon is filled when the current desktop is tiled.
  • Full-screen Spaces can't be tiled.

07 · Cheat sheet
Forgot a key? Press ⌃⌘/

A translucent panel lists every shortcut, grouped, on the display you're working on. It also works from the menu: Keyboard Shortcuts.

It never takes focus, so the app underneath keeps your keyboard and selection.

It closes on Esc, a click anywhere, a desktop switch, or the same shortcut again. On an untiled desktop the tiling rows are dimmed.

08 · Mouse and floating
The mouse still works, and Tiler follows it.
01

Drag onto a tile

Drop a window on another tile and the two swap places. Drop it anywhere else and it snaps back into its tile.

02

Drag an edge

Resize a window by its edge and the split follows: the neighbour shrinks or grows to match, gaps stay even.

03

Drop in, drag out

Move a window onto a tiled desktop and it joins the layout. Drag it to another display and Tiler lets it go.

Float one window: ⌃⌘T takes it out of the layout and centres it at 60 % of the screen. Press again to tile it.
Floats on its own: dialogs, panels, non-resizable windows, and apps on the Always Float list (System Settings, Activity Monitor, Calculator by default).

11 · Good to know
The config file, a few limits, and how to leave.
01

One config file

Settings writes ~/Library/Application Support/Tiler/config.json. Prefer a text editor? Edit it, then Settings › General › Reload.

Every key is required. A mistake is shown in the menu and in Settings, which stays read-only until it's fixed or you Restore Defaults.

02

Known limits

Apps with a minimum size (Outlook needs 770 pt) can overlap their neighbour on a small screen.

If another tool owns a key (Rectangle, Hammerspoon), it wins: pick another key in Settings. Don't tile the same desktop with HyprMac.

03

Uninstall

brew uninstall --cask tiler removes the app and keeps your settings.

brew uninstall --zap --cask tiler also removes the config and the list of tiled desktops.
